❯ The Brief
  Neurotype engineering take-home | Report Review Service | 1 of 2
  The setup
  A clinician works through a structured neurodevelopmental assessment with a
  young person. The output is a report: a
  rating for each domain, and a written summary. Once a reviewing clinician is
  happy, it is issued, and copies go to the
  family, the school and the GP. This is a slice of what we build. The
  instrument and scoring are invented: this page is the
  only truth.
  The constraints
  A Python backend and a React + TypeScript frontend, coming up with docker
  compose up on a machine that has only
  Docker, serving the app on :5173 and answering 200 on :8000/health. Use the
  harness we sent, or do it yourself.
  Everything else is your choice.
  The data
  Assessments come out of the clinician app as JSON, one per line. We have sent
  you assessments.jsonl, a sample of a
  hundred. One line, spread out to read, with most of the items and all but one
  domain left out:
  {
   "assessment_id": "a-00001", "client": { "date_of_birth": "2014-03-02",
  "nhs_number": "999 476 5919", "guardian_contact": "j.okafor@example.com",
  "safeguarding_notes": "Contact via mother only. Father not to be given
  dates." }, "assessed_at": "2026-03-02T09:30:00+00:00", "clinician_id":
  "c-005", "domains": [ { "domain": "social_communication", "items": [ {
  "code": "SC1", "raw": 12, "max": 20, "completed": true }, { "code": "SC2",
  "raw": null, "max": 20, "completed": false } ]} ], "summary": "Free text, up
  to 8000 characters." }
  Five domains, three to six items each: social_communication,
  sensory_processing, executive_function,
  emotional_regulation, motor_coordination. An item can be not completed, where
  the young person was unable or
  unwilling. A hundred is a sample. The live service holds years of them and
  takes on more every week.
  The scoring
  None of the following is in the file. Each domain gets a percentage, the mean
  of raw / max * 100 across its items, and
  from that a support-need band: Minimal 0 to 39, Mild 40 to 54, Moderate 55 to
  84, Substantial 85 to 100. A report also
  shows the young person's age in years and months at the date of assessment,
  and a review flag: on when any domain
  bands Substantial, any item went uncompleted, or the summary runs under 200
  characters.
  What we need
  Reviewing clinicians work a queue of assessments, drawn from everything the
  service holds. A reviewer needs to see
  that queue and cut it down: by how an assessment scored, by whether it is
  flagged, and by which clinician ran it.
  Opening one shows the whole thing: the client, the results per domain, the
  summary.
  When a reviewer is satisfied, they issue the report. Actually delivering the
  copies is somebody else's problem, not yours.
  Once issued it is fixed, because those copies are already out. Clinicians do
  sometimes also need to correct the
  occasional typo in the summary afterwards, and there is no clean way to do
  that today. The percentages, bands and flag
  are values we calculate and are subject to change.
  Client records get audited. We must be able to show an auditor who looked at
  what, when, and what changed. Tests are
  your call: tell us what you thought worth testing.
  More here than four hours holds, deliberately, so here is the bar. If you get
  one thing working, make it this: a
  reviewer opens the queue, narrows it, opens an assessment, and issues the
  report, running end to end. A narrow
  path that works beats a broad one that does not. Write down what you left and
  why; the second document says
  where

  here's the brief for this project, get the details you need from this. We'll
  start to build stuff out soon. As a practice, never assume anything. If you
  don't know something ask me, when I ask about a new change interview me to
  get the details you need. Try to only keep the details you need, don't add

❯ docker compose down -v 2>&1 | tail -10 what's the -v 2 bit about

● That's actually two separate things run together — not a single -v 2 flag:

  - -v — a docker compose down flag meaning "also remove named volumes." I used
    it to force a truly clean slate (wipe the Postgres data) before testing the
    fresh-load behavior.
  - 2>&1 — shell redirection, unrelated to Docker. It means "send stream 2
    (stderr) to wherever stream 1 (stdout) is currently going." Combined with
    the trailing | tail -10, it means: merge stderr into stdout, then pipe
    everything through tail -10 so only the last 10 lines print — mainly so
    build/teardown noise doesn't flood the output.

  So it reads as docker compose down -v, then 2>&1 | tail -10 tacked on for
  output control, not -v 2.
